# Live Preview

The preview renders your document to HTML **in your browser**, using the official PreTeXt XSLT stylesheets compiled to WebAssembly — no build server involved. The first preview in a session takes a moment while the renderer loads; after that, renders take a fraction of a second.

## When it updates

The preview follows you as you type: about a second after you pause, it re-renders and swaps in only what changed, keeping your place on the page. It also refreshes when you switch divisions, when you save (`Ctrl`/`Cmd`+`S`), when you press `Ctrl`/`Cmd`+`Enter`, and when you click **Refresh**.

## What it shows

The preview shows the division you are editing, rendered in the context of the whole project — so its numbering matches the built document and cross-references into other divisions resolve.

A banner across the top is a reminder that this is a live preview, not a full build. Your built output may look different: the preview does not apply your [Build settings](/building/build-settings/) such as the theme, and images generated by the PreTeXt toolchain — TikZ (`latex-image`) and Sage plots, for example — appear only in a real [build](/building/outputs/).

The preview starts in light mode and has its own readability menu, including a theme switch. That choice persists across rebuilds.

### Worksheets and handouts

When the division contains worksheets or handouts, a **Web view** menu appears in the preview's header. Choose a printout under **Print preview** to see it laid out on paper, with each workspace at the height you asked for.

### Slideshows

For a [slideshow](/writing/slideshows/), the header offers **Deck** — the whole deck as one scrolling page — and **Present** — one slide at a time, with pauses, as it will be presented — plus **−** and **+** to zoom.

## Two-way sync

Click anything in the preview and the cursor moves to the matching source line. Move the cursor and the preview scrolls to the matching element. Clicking content from another division opens *that* division and lands on the right line of its own source.

Line-level sync works for PreTeXt divisions. LaTeX and Markdown are converted before rendering, so clicking their content opens the right division but cannot place the cursor on a particular line.

## If a render fails

Most often this means the document is not well-formed mid-edit. The last good preview stays on screen, and a dismissible **Preview not updated** banner says what went wrong — for malformed XML, including the line to look at.

## Browser requirements

In-browser rendering needs WebAssembly JSPI, which Chromium-based browsers (Chrome, Edge) ship. In other browsers the preview is built on the server instead: it refreshes only when you save, press `Ctrl`/`Cmd`+`Enter`, or click **Refresh**, and it has no two-way sync.
